📚 Understanding Penny Stocks

⚠️ Why Penny Stocks Are Risky

Penny stocks (typically priced under $5) are shares of small companies with low market capitalisation. They trade on thin margins, are easily manipulated, often have poor financials, and can become worthless overnight. Most retail traders who actively trade penny stocks lose money.

📊 Why Volume Matters

Volume is how many shares changed hands in a session. High volume means easier entry and exit — you can buy or sell without moving the price much. Low-volume stocks have wide bid/ask spreads, making them expensive to trade and hard to exit when a position goes against you.

💧 Why Liquidity Matters

Liquidity is the ease of converting a position to cash at a fair price. Illiquid penny stocks may have large hidden costs: you might buy at $1.05 and immediately only be able to sell at $0.95. Relative Volume (RVOL) compares today's volume to the 20-day average — elevated RVOL often precedes price movement.

📉 Not Financial Advice

This dashboard scores stocks using free, potentially delayed data and deterministic rules. It does not know your tax situation, risk tolerance, or financial goals. No score guarantees a profitable trade. Never invest money you cannot afford to lose. This is a research and learning tool — nothing more.

🔍 How Scoring Works

Each ticker earns up to 100 points across three sub-scores: Momentum (price trend, RSI, moving averages), Liquidity (volume and RVOL), and Risk (volatility, price range). Bonuses apply for positive gaps; penalties apply for missing data or extreme volatility. A higher score means more signals align — not a guaranteed outcome.

🧐 Always Review Manually

Before acting on any candidate, read recent SEC filings, check for news catalysts, verify the company still operates, and look for dilution risk (shares outstanding / authorised). Automated signals cannot replace due diligence. Treat every recommendation as the start of research, not the end.
