Scrum Transition Course

Agile for software teams moving to Scrum on October 1

A practical three-week course designed to help team leads and developers learn the same concepts at different depth while transitioning from informal standups and ad hoc work tracking into a visible, repeatable Scrum system.

3 weeks 6 sessions Azure DevOps TFVC + shelvesets October 1 launch
Review the schedule Start with Session 1

Course purpose

This training exists for two audiences at once: team leads need enough depth to facilitate Scrum and teach the team, while developers need tactical guidance on what changes in daily work.

For team leads

  • Explain the purpose and lifecycle of a sprint.
  • Facilitate backlog refinement, planning, and retrospectives.
  • Coach the team through flow, ownership, and review quality.

For developers

  • Understand what changes on October 1.
  • Work from a visible backlog and board.
  • Use shelvesets, work-item association, and UAT in the Scrum flow.

Shared expectation

  • Agile does not remove design review, code review, UAT, or release control.
  • It places those controls inside a shorter, visible, iterative workflow.

September to October 1 timeline

Week of Sep 7–11

Sessions 1–2: establish the Scrum model, clarify ownership, and make Azure Boards work visible.

Week of Sep 14–18

Sessions 3–4: practice backlog refinement, forecasting, and sprint planning.

Week of Sep 21–25

Sessions 5–6: run the sprint, improve standups, and apply TFVC review and Definition of Done.

Sep 28–30

Launch readiness: confirm backlog, board states, roles, Definition of Done, and support coverage.

October 1

Official start of backlog, board, sprint tracking, and team-level Scrum operating rhythm.

How this fits the program

The Phased Sprint Delivery Plan adds an Agile team layer to existing contract and governance accountability. These are complementary layers, not competing processes.

Program level

Program Managers protect contract scope, deliverables, resources, risks, and reporting. The Government Lead / TPOC directs approved priorities at the epic and sprint level.

Team level

Team Leads coordinate ceremonies, feature progress, technical review, defect management, board health, and day-to-day execution. Developers own their stories and work items daily.

Traceability

Use the Azure DevOps hierarchy: Epic → Feature → User Story / Work Item. Every deliverable should trace back to an approved program objective.

Scope rule: document every request in Azure DevOps, assess it, route it to the Government Lead for decision, and begin only after approval is recorded. Major efforts require epic-level review; urgency does not bypass change control.

Lead Prep vs Team Session

Lead Prep — Learn It

Leads learn the reasoning, facilitation flow, terminology, and common traps before they teach the team. This is the deeper study layer.

  • Background and rationale
  • Common mistakes and tradeoffs
  • Facilitation prompts and leadership questions

Team Session — Teach It

Developers get a 45–60 minute lunch-and-learn focused on what changes in the way work is planned, tracked, reviewed, and accepted.

  • Objectives and examples from our environment
  • Hands-on practice or discussion
  • Rules, takeaways, and key facilitator questions

Quick reference glossary

Agile

A mindset centered on iterative value delivery, learning, and adaptation.

Scrum

A lightweight Agile framework with roles, events, and artifacts.

Sprint

A short, time-boxed period in which planned work is completed and reviewed.

Sprint Goal

The specific value or outcome the team intends to deliver in a sprint.

Product Backlog

The ordered list of work needed to deliver and improve the product.

Sprint Backlog

The subset of work selected and committed to within the sprint.

Increment

The sum of completed work at a point in time that is potentially releasable.

Definition of Done

The shared standard for when work is truly complete.

Story Points

A relative estimate of effort, complexity, and uncertainty—not a personal score.

Velocity

The rate at which a team delivers relative points over time.

Daily Scrum

A time-boxed check-in to inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt.

TFVC shelveset

A reviewable, check-in-safe way to share work before formal check-in in TFVC.

Lead readiness checklist

  • Can I explain the purpose of a sprint?
  • Can I explain the Sprint Goal?
  • Can I distinguish Product Backlog from Sprint Backlog?
  • Can I facilitate refinement and sprint planning?
  • Can I run a useful Daily Scrum?
  • Can I explain what happens to unfinished work?
  • Can I handle urgent work during a sprint?
  • Can I distinguish Sprint Review, UAT, and Retrospective?
  • Can I describe our Definition of Done?
  • Can I explain how TFVC code review fits the workflow?
  • Can I explain why story points are not an individual performance metric?

Key course principle

Agile is not a replacement for design review, code review, UAT, or release control. It places those quality gates inside a shorter, visible, iterative workflow so the team learns earlier and reduces risk sooner.
