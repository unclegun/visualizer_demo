A compact preparation and facilitation guide for team leads teaching the six-session transition with Azure DevOps and TFVC.

Before each session

  1. Read the matching Lead Prep tab and write down one local example.
  2. Choose a real but non-sensitive work item or scenario.
  3. Prepare the Azure Boards or paper board view.
  4. Identify the decision the exercise should produce.
  5. Keep the team discussion within 45–60 minutes.

Facilitator stance

  • Ask before telling; make decisions explicit.
  • Protect the Sprint Goal without hiding scope changes.
  • Coach the team; do not turn ceremonies into status reports.
  • Inspect systems and handoffs before judging individuals.
  • Park deep technical discussions with a named owner.

Readiness checklist

  • Can I explain the purpose of a sprint and Sprint Goal?
  • Can I distinguish Product Backlog from Sprint Backlog?
  • Can I facilitate refinement and planning?
  • Can I run a board-driven Daily Scrum in 15 minutes?
  • Can I explain unfinished work, urgent work, and capacity tradeoffs?
  • Can I distinguish Sprint Review, UAT, release, and Retrospective?
  • Can I explain our Definition of Done and TFVC shelveset review?
  • Can I explain why story points are not individual performance metrics?

Governance to reinforce

  • Program Manager protects contract scope, deliverables, resources, risks, reporting, and formal contractor escalation.
  • Government Lead / TPOC directs macro priorities within approved scope and reviews epic/sprint progress.
  • Team Lead coordinates ceremonies, feature progress, board health, technical review, and risk escalation.
  • Developer owns daily user-story updates, estimates, blockers, and early communication.

Change control: document, assess, review, decide, then execute. Major efforts do not begin until the Government Lead decision is documented.

Situation prompts

Urgent production bug

Make it a visible work item. Clarify urgency and impact, then adjust capacity, forecast, or Sprint Goal with the team and priority owner.

Customer request mid-sprint

Capture it in the Product Backlog. Do not silently add scope; discuss what should leave if the new work is more important.

UAT rejection

Keep the item out of Done, record the acceptance gap, and inspect whether the goal or plan needs adaptation.

Team misses the goal

Inspect assumptions, dependencies, capacity, and slicing. Treat the retrospective as a process learning space, not a performance hearing.

Opening question for every session: “What decision should the team be able to make differently after this hour?”
