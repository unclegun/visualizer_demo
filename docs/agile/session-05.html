Session 5

Running the Sprint, Daily Scrum, Risks, and Escalation

Lead prep: 45–60 minTeam session: 45–60 min
Why it matters: Early communication gives the team options; late communication creates emergencies.

Standup purpose

Scrum Practice The Daily Scrum inspects progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapts the next day’s plan. Program Rule It is required, board-driven, about 15 minutes, and is team coordination—not a management report, technical walkthrough, performance evaluation, optional meeting, or place to hide problems.

Facilitate flow

Start with the Sprint Goal and walk work right-to-left where useful: finish before starting. Ask what moved, what is blocked, which dependency needs help, and whether an item is aging. Park troubleshooting after standup. Ensure cards move when actual state changes and blockers are documented the same day.

Risk and escalation

Program Rule Categories are Schedule, Technical, Scope, and Dependency. Escalation is Developer → Team Lead → Government TPOC / Program Manager as appropriate. Team Leads also conduct weekly board/backlog reviews, coach, and escalate In Progress work with no update after five business days.

Failure modes, scenarios, self-check, coaching key

  • Status round replaces work coordination.
  • Deep debugging consumes the meeting.
  • Blocked work remains In Progress.
  • Aging or schedule risk is softened until it is urgent.
  • New work starts without a scope decision.

Scenario: an aging item, technical blocker, dependency, review-ready item, and possible schedule miss appear together. What moves, what is flagged, who owns follow-up, and what is escalated?

Ask: What is standup for? Why right-to-left? When is a blocker documented? Name four risk categories. Who receives escalation? What belongs after standup?

Key: coordinate toward the goal; finish work; same day; Schedule/Technical/Scope/Dependency; Team Lead then Government/TPOC or PM; troubleshooting.

Objectives and concept (10–15 minutes)

  • Run a board-driven standup in about 15 minutes.
  • Surface blockers and risks honestly.
  • Separate coordination from troubleshooting.

Program Rule Update ADO every business day, move actual states, and document blockers same day.

Exercise (20–25 minutes): standup simulation

Use a board containing an aging item, technical blocker, dependency, review-ready item, and possible schedule miss. Walk right-to-left, update states, flag the blocker, assign follow-up/escalation, and park technical discussion outside the meeting.

Facilitator prompts

  • What can we finish before pulling more?
  • What threatens the Sprint Goal?
  • What is the next state?
  • Who owns the follow-up and by when?

Takeaways and knowledge check

Ask: Is standup a status report? What does right-to-left protect? When do you record a blocker? What are the four risk categories? Who escalates?

Answer: no; finishing flow; same day; Schedule, Technical, Scope, Dependency; Developer to Team Lead to Government TPOC/PM as appropriate.
