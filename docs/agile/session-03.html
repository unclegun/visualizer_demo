Session 3

Backlogs, Work Items, Refinement, and Board Standards

Lead prep: 45–60 minTeam session: 45–60 min
Why it matters: A board is useful only when it represents approved work clearly enough to prioritize, discuss, review, and accept.

Concept and purpose

Scrum Practice The Product Backlog is ordered potential work; the Sprint Backlog is the selected work and plan. Program Rule Our ADO hierarchy is Epic → Feature → User Story / Work Item, and every item traces to an approved program objective.

Refinement reduces uncertainty before planning: clarify outcome, acceptance criteria, dependencies, estimates, and a slice that can move through the local workflow.

Hierarchy and two-tier boards

LevelMeaningOwner
EpicContract deliverable, major objective, government priorityGovernment Lead reviews status
FeatureCapability, functional requirement, planned releaseTeam Lead manages progress
Story / Work ItemTask, enhancement, defect, technical workDeveloper updates daily

Program Rule Feature/request board: Backlog → In Progress → In Review → Done. Task board: Backlog → In Progress → Done with a Blocked flag.

Standards and failure modes

  • Acceptance criteria exist before In Progress.
  • Update every business day and move cards when reality changes.
  • Document blockers the same day.
  • Escalate In Progress items with no update after five business days.
  • Board Health Test: anyone can see what is active, blocked and why, recently completed, and next.

Common failures: hidden work, vague “improve” items, horizontal slices, stale cards, and treating technical debt, spikes, sustainment, or defects as unofficial work.

Facilitation, scenarios, and self-check

Ask “What outcome can a user or operator verify?” and “What must be true for acceptance?” Do not let a Definition of Ready become a bureaucracy gate.

  1. Classify “fix invalid date response.”
  2. Trace a reporting enhancement to its Epic.
  3. Write two acceptance criteria for a login change.
  4. Split an oversized database/UI request vertically.
  5. What does the five-day rule require?
  6. What belongs on the board?
  7. What does Done mean on each board?

Coaching key: bug; link through Feature to approved Epic; criteria must be observable; split by user value; Team Lead escalates stale work; all meaningful work and risk; feature Done is accepted/verified and task Done is complete with the local evidence.

Objectives and concept (10–15 minutes)

  • Classify work and preserve Epic traceability.
  • Write acceptance criteria before work starts.
  • Use the two-tier board and pass the Board Health Test.

Program Rule ADO is the visible source of truth; update every business day and record blockers same day.

Exercise (20–25 minutes)

Classify five cards as story, task, bug, spike, technical debt, or sustainment; trace each to an Epic; write acceptance criteria; split an oversized item; place each on the correct board state. One learner runs the Board Health Test.

Facilitator prompts

  • What outcome is visible to the customer?
  • Can UAT verify this criterion?
  • Is this approved and prioritized?
  • What dependency or blocker must be visible?

Takeaways and knowledge check

Ask: What is the hierarchy? When may work enter In Progress? What are the feature states? What does Blocked mean? What happens after five stale business days?

Answer: Epic → Feature → Story/Work Item; after acceptance criteria and approval; Backlog/In Progress/In Review/Done; a visible impediment; Team Lead escalation.
