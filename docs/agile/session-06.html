Session 6

Quality Flow, Sprint Closeout, and Full Simulation

Why it mattersLead prep: 60 minTeam session: 60 min
Why it matters: The final session makes the full Phased Sprint Delivery Plan tangible: quality controls remain, work stays visible, and the team practices adapting when reality changes.

Quality flow and Definition of Done

Associate the Azure DevOps work item, develop, create a TFVC shelveset, complete Team Lead review, check in correctly, provide validation evidence, and complete UAT where required. The Definition of Done includes acceptance criteria, code completion, work-item association, review, check-in, build, required validation, UAT where applicable, and accurate board status. Unit tests and CI remain future maturity goals.

Closeout conversations

  • Sprint Review: demonstrate the completed increment and gather stakeholder feedback.
  • UAT: confirm acceptance expectations; it is not the same as the Sprint Review.
  • Release / Go Live: deploy when operationally ready, even if that is independent of the sprint boundary.
  • Retrospective: inspect the system and choose only one or two concrete improvements.

Facilitator coaching

When a story grows, split or reforecast. When production work arrives, make it a visible item and show the capacity tradeoff. When UAT rejects work or review finds issues, keep it out of Done. When the Sprint Goal is threatened, inspect dependencies, scope, and capacity before attributing individual blame.

Learning objectives

  • Practice the complete board, TFVC, review, UAT, and closeout flow.
  • Make scope and capacity tradeoffs visible.
  • Finish with a Sprint Review and one or two retrospective actions.

Workflow simulation

Refinement→Planning→Standup→Development→Sustainment issue→Board update→Shelveset→Lead review→UAT→Review→Retro

Reveal one scenario at a time

  1. Refine three items and agree on a Sprint Goal.
  2. Plan for three developers, PTO, meetings, and the 50/25/25 capacity model.
  3. Run a board-driven standup and identify a dependency.
  4. Reveal an urgent production bug. Decide what changes and document it in ADO.
  5. Move a feature to Shelveset, perform Team Lead review, and check in.
  6. Reveal UAT acceptance or rejection; decide whether the item is Done.
  7. Demonstrate the increment and choose one or two retrospective actions.

Key rules / takeaways

  • Do not start unapproved requests; use the documented change-control path.
  • Keep ADO current every business day and record blockers immediately.
  • Review, UAT, release, and retrospective serve different purposes.
  • Inspect process and system conditions before judging performance.
