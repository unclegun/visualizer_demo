Course context

Phased Sprint Delivery Plan

The governance context behind this Agile transition: program accountability and team-level Scrum work together to answer different questions.

Two complementary levels

Program level

Managed by the Program Manager. Contract deliverables, milestones, government reporting, risk management, scope adherence, resource management, and the contractor escalation path.

Question: Are we delivering the agreed-upon outcomes?

Team level (Agile)

Managed day to day by Team Leads and developers. Sprint execution, standups, backlog and feature progress, change control, work-item tracking, technical reviews, defect management, and continuous improvement.

Question: How are we completing the work?

Board standards

Feature/request board

Backlog → In Progress → In Review → Done. This supports status reporting and government visibility.

  • ADO is the single source of truth.
  • Update every business day.
  • Move cards when the real status changes.
  • Acceptance criteria exist before In Progress.

Task-level execution

Teams may use Backlog → In Progress → Done beneath each feature, with a Blocked flag for daily execution.

  • Document blockers the same day.
  • Escalate items In Progress more than five business days without an update.
  • The board should answer what is active, blocked, complete, and next.

Roles and escalation

RoleCourse emphasis
Program ManagerProtects contract scope, staffing/resources, reporting, and formal contractor escalation.
Government Lead / TPOCDirects priorities within approved scope, reviews epic/sprint progress, and confirms government expectations.
Team LeadFacilitates Scrum Master activities, leads technical review, maintains board health, and escalates risks to Government Lead and PM.
DeveloperOwns work items, estimates, daily ADO updates, refinement participation, and early risk communication.
Escalate immediately: schedule, technical, scope, and dependency risks go from Team Lead to Government Lead / TPOC and Program Manager. Early communication gives the team options; late communication creates emergencies.

Scope and capacity rules

  1. Document: add every request to ADO with requestor, description, date, and estimate.
  2. Assess: Team Lead evaluates schedule, resource, and priority impact.
  3. Review: route it to the Government Lead before work begins.
  4. Decide: approve, defer, or reject, with the decision recorded in ADO.
  5. Execute: update backlog and sprint priorities only after approval.

Capacity planning: use the program allocation of 50% major work, 25% minor work, and 25% meetings/administration, then account for PTO and sustainment support.

Major efforts require epic-level review and the full phased delivery process. Scope discipline applies to changes to existing functionality as well as new features.

Official metrics cover delivery (completed work, WIP, backlog size, sprint completion), quality (defects, rework, review cycle time), process (blockers, aging, ADO compliance, training), and program (deliverables, milestones, scope-change volume, risk trends).

Program Rule Metrics are process and delivery indicators, never punitive individual scores. Story points must never rank developers.

Engineering Practice: local Definition of Done

  • Acceptance criteria satisfied and code complete
  • ADO work item accurate and change associated with it
  • Team Lead review complete and correct TFVC check-in complete
  • Application builds and required validation is complete
  • UAT complete where required and board reflects completion

Future Maturity Unit tests, CI, automated quality gates, and sophisticated branching are future improvements, not current requirements.
