Sep 28–30 · Launch readiness

Ready for the October 1 operational launch

This is readiness work, not Session 7. Use the checklist with the Team Lead, Government Lead/TPOC, developers, customer, and UAT owner.

Readiness checklist

Board

  • ☐ Epic → Feature → Work Item hierarchy ready
  • ☐ Approved work populated and traceable
  • ☐ Acceptance criteria present before In Progress
  • ☐ Feature states: Backlog → In Progress → In Review → Done
  • ☐ Task board has Backlog → In Progress → Done and Blocked flag
  • ☐ Ownership and blocker reasons visible

Sprint

  • ☐ Sprint Goal agreed
  • ☐ 50/25/25 allocation visible
  • ☐ PTO, support, meetings, and dependencies included
  • ☐ Backlog refined and estimates recorded
  • ☐ Scope approvals visible in ADO

Team

  • ☐ Daily ADO updates and same-day blockers understood
  • ☐ 15-minute board-driven standup rehearsed
  • ☐ Escalation path understood
  • ☐ TFVC shelveset and Team Lead review practiced
  • ☐ UAT ownership and acceptance path confirmed

Governance

  • ☐ Government Lead priorities confirmed
  • ☐ Scope approval is visible
  • ☐ Team Lead Governance Review understood
  • ☐ Bi-weekly Program Review and Monthly Government Status Review understood
  • ☐ ADO metrics/dashboard ready

Rehearse one real or sanitized item

Epic→Feature→Work Item→Refinement→Sprint→Board→Development→Shelveset→Lead Review→Check-in→UAT→Done→Release

At each step name the owner, evidence, ADO update, and decision. Keep TFVC terminology; this is not a Git or PR workflow.

Pressure-test the operating model

Simulate three events

  1. Urgent request: document, assess, review with Government Lead, record approve/defer/reject, then update scope.
  2. Blocker: flag it, document the dependency the same day, assign follow-up, and escalate Developer → Team Lead → Government TPOC/Program Manager.
  3. Missed estimate: update ADO, inspect capacity and Sprint Goal, and reforecast—never hide the miss or score a developer.
Launch gate: Go live on October 1 only when the board, sprint, team, and governance checks are owned and evidenced. Success means risks surface early, ADO is current, unauthorized work does not start, and government has data-driven visibility.
