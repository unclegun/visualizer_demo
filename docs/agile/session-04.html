Session 4

Estimation and Sprint Planning

Why it mattersLead prep: 45–60 minTeam session: 45–60 min
Why it matters: Planning should create a credible forecast around a Sprint Goal, not a promise that every uncertain item will finish.

Forecasting with capacity and evidence

Capacity accounts for three developers per team, PTO, meetings, support, and known sustainment load. Relative points such as 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, and 13 express size, complexity, and uncertainty. Velocity is a team trend used for forecasting, not a target or individual productivity score.

Never rank developers by points. That destroys estimation honesty and encourages work splitting for appearance rather than value.

Planning flow

  1. Clarify the Product Backlog order and Sprint Goal.
  2. Review acceptance criteria, dependencies, and design review needs.
  3. Estimate collaboratively using planning poker or a quick relative comparison.
  4. Check capacity, including expected production support.
  5. Select a coherent forecast; negotiate scope if an urgent item arrives.

Edge cases

  • Unfinished work returns to the Product Backlog for re-plioritization; it is not automatically counted as complete.
  • Urgent sustainment work is added transparently and displaces or changes the forecast.
  • A 13-point item is a signal to split or investigate, not a challenge to “work harder.”

Lead questions

  • What outcome makes these items a coherent Sprint Goal?
  • What support load would make this forecast unrealistic?
  • What is our confidence level, and what evidence would change it?

Learning objectives

  • Use simple relative estimates without pretending they are hours.
  • Build a mock two-week sprint from priority and capacity.
  • Handle PTO, support work, and unfinished items honestly.

Concept explanation

Estimate as a team with 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, or 13. Compare items to a shared reference. The number helps the team see uncertainty; it does not measure a person. Planning chooses a forecast that supports the Sprint Goal within the capacity available.

Exercise: mock sprint

Give the group a sample backlog: a 5-point UI enhancement, 3-point database correction, 8-point customer workflow, 2-point production bug, and 3-point design spike. Remove one developer-day for PTO and reserve time for support. Estimate, split the 8-point item if needed, then select a forecast and Sprint Goal.

Key rules / questions

  • Are we choosing by priority plus capacity?
  • Which item most supports the Sprint Goal?
  • What will we do if urgent support consumes capacity?
  • Are we treating points as team planning information, never a personal score?
