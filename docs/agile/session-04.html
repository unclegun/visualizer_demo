Session 4

Estimation, Capacity, Sprint Planning, and Change Control

Lead prep: 45–60 minTeam session: 45–60 min
Why it matters: A forecast is a transparent tradeoff between priority, capacity, and uncertainty—not a guarantee or a personal quota.

Concept, why, and principle

Agile Principle Forecast from evidence and adapt when assumptions change. Scrum Practice A Sprint Goal, relative estimates, and velocity support a team forecast; story points never rank developers.

Program Capacity Allocation

Program Capacity Allocation Reserve 50% Major work, 25% Minor work, 25% meetings/admin, then account for PTO, sustainment/support, dependencies, and production issues. This is a local planning rule, not Scrum.

If urgent work arrives, show what capacity or scope changes. Do not silently add it.

Change control and edge cases

  1. Document: requestor, description, date, estimate.
  2. Assess: Team Lead evaluates schedule, resource, and priority impact.
  3. Review: Team Lead brings the assessment to Government Lead.
  4. Decision: Government Lead approves, defers, or rejects; record it in ADO.
  5. Execute: update backlog/sprint and start approved work.

Implementation tasks already inside an approved sprint do not each need Government Lead approval. A sprint-level scope change or new Epic does; major efforts may span multiple sprints and use the full phased process. Seniority and urgency do not bypass governance.

Failure modes, scenarios, self-check, coaching key

  • Points become individual targets.
  • Meetings/PTO/support are omitted.
  • Urgent work is hidden in chat.
  • Unfinished work is counted as complete.
  • Minor execution is confused with a new major effort.

Ask: What is the Sprint Goal? What capacity is real? Is this execution, a sprint change, or a new Epic? What decision must be recorded? How will unfinished work be reforecast?

Key: choose a coherent team forecast; reserve 50/25/25 and actual constraints; document and route changes; record approval; return unfinished work for re-prioritization.

Objectives and concept (10–15 minutes)

  • Estimate relatively with 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13.
  • Build a Sprint Goal from priority and real capacity.
  • Make scope/capacity tradeoffs explicit.

Exercise (20–25 minutes): mock sprint

Plan Major work, Minor work, meetings/admin, PTO, support, and an urgent request. Apply 50/25/25, estimate as a team, split an oversized item, then decide what is deferred. Document the request and the Government Lead decision in the scenario ADO record.

Facilitator prompts

  • What outcome makes this a Sprint Goal?
  • What displaces work when support consumes capacity?
  • Is this inside approved scope?
  • Are points being used as a team planning signal?

Takeaways and knowledge check

Ask: What is a forecast? What is the local allocation? Who approves sprint-level change? What is a major effort? What happens to unfinished work?

Answer: evidence-based team expectation; 50/25/25 plus constraints; Government Lead after assessment; documented Epic-level effort; return and re-prioritize.
